# Landmark validation (Phase 4) - what was measured

Tool: `python -m evals.landmark_validation --image <path> --label X`. Tests: `tests/test_landmark_map.py` (always run) and
`tests/test_frontalize_and_landmarks.py` (real-photo part runs only with `PFI_TEST_IMAGE=path[,path]`).
**No photograph is stored in the repository.** Two photographs supplied by the project owner for this testing were read from
their original location; fixtures are generated by transforming a photo at test time.

## What this is NOT
There is **no ground-truth annotation** of landmarks, so **landmark accuracy (error against a human or reference) has not
been measured.** Everything below measures stability (how much landmarks move when the input changes) and internal
consistency. n = 2 photographs of 2 adult men with beards; results do not generalise to other faces, skin tones, ages,
cameras or lighting.

## Left/right pair table checked against the real model
Mirror each photo; every R/L pair must swap and x must reflect. Error in % of inter-pupil distance (IPD):
- correct table: max 2.6 (photo A) and 3.21 (photo B); mean 1.57 and 1.8
- if the pairs were NOT swapped (photo A): min 43.99, mean 132.2

So the mapping is right, and the model's own left/right inconsistency is ~1.6-1.8% of IPD on average. For scale, IPD is
about 0.45 of face width, so this is roughly 0.7-0.8% of face width: **the symmetry metric (percent of face width) cannot
resolve differences much below that** (single-subject estimate).

## Regression thresholds (tests)
They are **engineering regression guards**, about 2x the largest value measured above, chosen so a pipeline change that
makes landmarks noticeably less stable fails a test. They are not accuracy claims. JPEG q95-q50: mean < 1.0, max < 3.0;
resize x0.5-x1.25: mean < 1.5, max < 4.0; roll up to 20 deg: mean < 2.0, max < 6.0; mirror pairs: max < 8; identical-input
runs: < 1e-6 px.

## `frontalize()` investigation
- **Why it saturated:** the search grid is +/-30 deg. The result is the rotation that best balances left/right pairs. At the
  edge the true turn (or the subject's own offset) exceeds the range; the minimum is then the grid boundary and, further
  out, the periodic imbalance wraps to the opposite side (photo A: applied turn 35 deg returns +30.55 deg, not -30).
  Separately, when depth is missing every rotation scores the same and the tie used to resolve to -30 deg.
- **Is the result still valid when saturated?** No. It is a *partial* rotation, so widths stay foreshortened; it must not be
  presented as a normal frontalised face.
- **Measured on real landmarks (photo A, base estimate -15.95 deg):** recovery of an additional applied yaw is exact (slope 1,
  0.00 deg error on a synthetic symmetric 3D face) until the sum reaches the limit: 13 deg extra -> -28.95 (ok), 15 deg ->
  -30.50 (saturated), 35 deg -> +30.55 (wrong side).
- **Fix (implemented):** `frontalize_with_status()` returns `ok | saturated | undetermined`; ties prefer the smallest rotation;
  flat/depthless input is `undetermined` with no rotation; `compute()` records `yaw_correction_status`; a saturated result
  suppresses the symmetry metric and flags width ratios. `frontalize()` keeps its old signature.
- **Range not widened:** beyond +/-30 deg the far side of the face is largely hidden, so the model's landmarks there are
  guesses and a larger rotation would not be trustworthy; widening was not validated and was not done. Rejecting such input is
  the correct behaviour, and the quality gate already errors above 15 deg of MediaPipe yaw.
- **Open inconsistency:** `frontalize`'s yaw estimate is larger than MediaPipe's pose yaw on both photos (-15.95 vs -11.12 deg;
  +7.6 vs +4.12 deg). With n = 2 the cause (genuine asymmetry absorbed by the balance criterion, or a definition difference) is
  not established. Because the gate accepts up to 15 deg of MediaPipe yaw, a photo near the gate limit could approach the
  +/-30 deg wall.

## Fixture plan and what cannot be tested yet
Synthesisable from one authorised photo (done): resolution, compression, roll, brightness, colour cast, blur, occlusion
boxes, mirror, two faces. **Needs captured, authorised photographs and is NOT covered:** real yaw and pitch variation (a 2D
transform is not a head turn), glasses, beard/clean-shaven pairs, expressions, real partial occlusion (hand, mask), real
low-quality phone images. For each captured fixture record: quality gate result, MediaPipe pose, expected capability
(which metrics should be available) and whether each metric is available; compare to the gate's actual output.
